About Nitish Kumar — Independent Researcher and DISHA Inventor

Some people enter public life through institutions. Others arrive through experience.

His work developed from questions about records, institutions, accountability, and the consequences of silence in the digital age. Not from a thesis, a grant, or a commission — from noticing things that did not add up, and staying with the questions long enough to document them properly.

The research approach combines constitutional law, cyber forensics, public policy, governance systems, AI, cybersecurity, disaster management, and digital infrastructure. The work draws from court records, parliamentary proceedings, audit reports, RTI disclosures, policy archives, demographic data, and publicly available evidence.

Professionally, he works with enterprise technology, cloud platforms, AI, cybersecurity, and digital transformation while independently researching the evolving relationship between citizens, institutions, and emerging technologies in India.

DISHA

From foundation sketch to mature architecture

DISHA was first conceptualised in 2012 as a foundation sketch for signals, intelligence, geography, knowledge, and decision-making. By 2026, it had evolved into a mature architecture for evidence, memory, digital constitutional personhood, and accountability.

Purpose

Why this work exists.

This work exists to examine how digital systems, public institutions, cyber infrastructure, and constitutional duties interact in real life.

It studies the gap between automated records and human remedy, between data collection and citizen protection, between governance claims and evidence. The archive is non-commercial, independent, and maintained as a public-interest documentary record.

Professional and public-interest work

Two tracks, one method.

Professional career: building and governing large technology systems — Microsoft Dynamics 365, Azure, AWS, cloud transformation, data platforms, applied AI. Independent research: applying the same discipline of evidence, controls, traceability, and accountable decisions to government dashboards, court filings, public records, and data flows.

DISHA is the framework connecting both tracks — a method for preserving evidence, testing claims, and keeping human accountability visible in a world of automated governance.

Method principles

  • Trace the system — Identify who controls the record, process, platform, or decision.
  • Preserve the record — Keep source paths, dates, correspondence, and procedural context visible.
  • Test the outcome — Compare institutional claims with evidence, delivery, remedy, and citizen impact.
