Finding 01 / Operation Anatomy

Eight-Step Forensic Breakdown of a Digital Arrest Operation

Digital arrest fraud is psychologically sophisticated terror-based extortion where criminals impersonating law enforcement (CBI, ED, NCB, TRAI, Customs, Supreme Court) conduct fake video interrogations lasting hours to days. In 2024, this single fraud category extracted Rs 2,140 crore from Indian victims. The Supreme Court confirmed in January 2025 that digital arrest has no legal existence in Indian law — no court can arrest you digitally.

StepStageMethodologyEvidence to Preserve
1Target identificationVictims selected from ICMR breach data (Aadhaar + mobile), TRAI consumer databases, income tax e-filing leaks, IRCTC records. Preferred: age 45–70, professional or retired, tier-1/tier-2 city.If contacted, note that the caller knew your details before you provided them — this itself is forensic evidence of breach data use.
2Pretext automated callIVR message: 'Your Aadhaar/mobile has been used to send illegal parcels. Press 1 to speak to an officer.' Victims who engage are transferred to human operators.Record the exact IVR text. Note the number displayed. Screenshot call log with timestamp. Check sancharsaathi.gov.in for the number.
3Video call setupWhatsApp/Skype/Zoom. Scammer in fake CBI/ED uniform. Professional backdrop: logo, flag, multiple screens showing victim's photo and 'case file'. Fake warrant number, FIR number provided verbally.Screenshot or record the video call (where lawful). Note the fake FIR number, warrant number, 'officer' name, badge details shown. These are fabricated but specific — useful for the FIR.
4Isolation commandCRITICAL: 'Do NOT tell anyone — not family, bank, or friends — or you will be immediately arrested under NDPS Act. Remain on camera.' Total psychological isolation enforced.Note the isolation instruction with exact words. Isolation is the primary psychological control mechanism — it is itself evidence of the fraud pattern.
5Escalation chainCall escalated to 'Senior Officers': ED Joint Director, Supreme Court Bench, RBI Deputy Governor. Victim shown fabricated documents: warrants, court orders, news articles with victim's name.Screenshot all fabricated documents shown. Note every 'authority' name used. Save any documents sent via WhatsApp or email — key forensic evidence.
6Money extraction'Pay Rs X as bail bond / investigation fee. This is temporary — returned after 48-hour RBI verification. Failure means immediate arrest of yourself and family members.'Screenshot every payment instruction. Note UPI ID, RTGS account, IFSC, crypto wallet address. Do NOT pay. If already paid, preserve all UTR numbers, transaction IDs, screenshots.
7Repeat extractionAfter first payment, new 'charges' discovered. Repeated until victim has no accessible funds. Coerced to take personal loans, sell gold, liquidate FDs, borrow from family.Document every transaction. Preserve loan documents taken under duress. Bank statements showing transfers are primary evidence. Contact 1930 immediately.
8Exit and erasureScammers disappear. Money routed through 6–10 mule accounts in under 15 minutes, converted to USDT Tether cryptocurrency, transferred to China/Cambodia-based wallets.File complaint at cybercrime.gov.in within 60 minutes of last transaction for best recovery chance. Preserve all digital evidence: call logs, screenshots, WhatsApp chats, bank records.
Operational base (I4C intelligence, 2024): Majority of digital arrest call centres operate from Myawaddy (Myanmar), Sihanoukville (Cambodia), and tech hubs in Kunming and Shenzhen (China). Indian nationals trafficked to these centres under fake job offers. MEA confirmed: 250+ Indian nationals rescued from Myanmar scam compounds in 2023–24. CBI arrested 11 domestic coordinators who recruited and sold Indians to Myanmar syndicates for $3,000–$10,000 per person (November 2024 chargesheet).

Finding 02 / Profile Dependency

Why the Victim Believes — The Data Profile as the Weapon

Digital arrest scams do not work through technical sophistication. They work because the fraudster already knows who the victim is — before the first call. The data is the weapon. Believing the caller was not naivety; it was a rational response to a caller who possessed private information that only a genuine authority should have had.

What the Fraudster KnewWhere It Came FromWhy It Made the Victim Believe
Your full nameICMR breach (815M records), Aadhaar ecosystem breach, telecom breachOnly a real authority 'should' know your name when calling
Your Aadhaar numberICMR breach, CoWIN breach, loan app KYC harvest, dark web purchaseAadhaar number is treated as a secret — knowing it signals legitimacy
Your phone is linked to your bankFinancial SMS harvested during loan app data collection; telecom breach'We can see your account activity' appears credible
Your home locationChinese app GPS data, adtech location profiling, telecom tower dataGeographical specificity removes doubt about who is calling
When you are home aloneAdtech location profiling — stationary, at home, in the eveningTiming optimised for maximum psychological vulnerability
Your psychological pressure pointsSocial media, search data, financial distress signals in adtech dataScammer knows what threatens you most: family arrest, job loss, public shame
AI escalation (2024–2026): From 2024, the profiles harvested from Chinese apps in 2020–21 gained a new capability layer. Voice cloning: audio samples from TikTok videos, Instagram reels, and WhatsApp voice messages are now sufficient to clone a person's voice — fraudsters impersonate family members in real-time. Deepfake video: face data from selfies is combined with AI video generation for real-time video call deepfakes. A Kerala man transferred money after a video call that appeared to show his friend in distress. This is the digital dacoit's final weapon — the elimination of verifiable reality.
