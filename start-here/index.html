What is supported by official records

Official facts should be traced to court records, government documents, RTI replies, CAG audit material, administrative correspondence or authenticated public records. The archive links each factual claim to its primary source document. Where a source document is available for download, the link is provided.

What has been submitted to authorities or courts

Petitions, representations and grievances are documented submissions. They should not be read as final findings unless an authority or court expressly records such a finding. W.P.(Crl.) 163/2026 was disposed 19 May 2026 with a direction to MeitY — that direction is procedural, not a ruling on the underlying facts.

What the judicial record says

Procedural court directions are identified as procedural unless the order itself adjudicates the underlying issue. The archive separates what a court said from what the petitioner contended and from what the evidence independently shows. See the relevant case pages in Intelligence.

The core research areas

Five areas, one public-interest line.

Article 12

Digital State power and constitutional accountability

Article 12 of the Constitution defines "the State" for the purpose of fundamental rights enforcement. The research examines whether digital government systems — databases, portals, outsourced infrastructure — exercise state-equivalent power over citizens while remaining outside the traditional Article 12 boundary. When a citizen's identity is in a database but their remedy is not, the constitutional question is structural, not administrative. Read the research.

Digital Constitutional Personhood

Citizen identity in the digital layer

Digital Constitutional Personhood is a proposed framework arguing that a citizen's legal identity should follow them into digital systems with the same constitutional protections that apply in physical space. A citizen who exists in Aadhaar, NPCI, GSTN, DigiLocker, and CERT-In databases but cannot assert a fundamental right in any of them has a personhood problem, not a technology problem. Read the framework.

DISHA Intelligence Architecture

Evidence-first civic intelligence

DISHA is the operational backbone of this archive — a six-layer intelligence framework that separates verified records from assessments, raw evidence from analysis, and documentary findings from petitioner contentions. Every case in this archive is structured through DISHA methodology: source-first, evidence-logged, and publicly accountable. Read the whitepaper.

Intelligence Archive

Case-level evidence records

The intelligence archive documents five public cases with source links, court references, government records, and timeline analysis: NDMA disaster-fund accountability (Case 01), MeitY digital governance and W.P.(Crl.) 163/2026 (Case 02), NITI Aayog certification and funds (Case 03), ODF false justification (Case 04), and the SIR constitutional process question (Case 12). A sixth case, Citizen Not Found, examines the structural gap in Digital India's citizen-centricity. Read the archive.

Cyber Evidence

Adtech, foreign servers, and data extraction

The archive covers adtech surveillance networks (SilverPush, InMobi, APUS Group), Chinese-linked cyber-fraud infrastructure, data held on foreign servers, and the jurisdictional gap in India's enforcement framework. These are not speculative claims — they are documented with technical records, forensic reports, government filings, and media-verified sources. Read the MeitY case.
