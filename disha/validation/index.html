DISHA Validation and Accuracy Basis

The Intelligence page on this site states that DISHA findings, measured against government records and open data, have held to an accuracy of 97% to date. This page explains what that figure means, how it is derived, and what it does not represent.

What the 97% measures

The 97% figure is a methodological accuracy measure, not a claim that every DISHA conclusion is a final adjudicated fact. It reflects the proportion of DISHA analytical findings that, when subsequently checked against independently available government records, audit reports, RTI replies, court filings, or published data, remained consistent with those documents.

In simple terms: where a DISHA case file draws an inference from primary records, that inference has been found to correspond with the documentary trail 97% of the time when the trail was later expanded or independently examined.

Finding categories

Every DISHA finding is classified into one of the following categories during validation review. The 97% figure counts findings in the first category as a proportion of all findings that have reached a reviewable state.

Consistent with source record

The DISHA assessment aligns with the independently verifiable documentary record. Counted within the 97%.

Partially supported

The assessment is partially borne out by records but some elements remain without direct documentary confirmation. Not counted within the 97%.

Incorrect or contradicted

The assessment is contradicted by source records. Not counted within the 97%.

Unresolved

No independent record yet available to confirm or contradict. Excluded from the denominator until a record becomes available.

Validation record

97% DISHA Accuracy — measured against government records, court materials, audit records, RTI trails and open data documented in the Intelligence Archive.

The published accuracy figure is an aggregate of findings assessed across the source-linked Intelligence Archive. Each live case file identifies its evidence class and links to the underlying records. A consolidated machine-readable claim ledger is being assembled from those published records.

Methodological limitations

  • The 97% figure reflects consistency with available records, not independent third-party audit.
  • The evaluation is conducted by the inventor, which requires every finding to be traceable to a specific source document — a standard documented on the Claim-to-Source System page.
  • Findings classified as "unresolved" are excluded from the calculation until independent records become available.
  • The accuracy measure covers analytical findings — inferences drawn from records — not the underlying records themselves. Government records, RTI replies, and court filings are primary sources; DISHA assessments are secondary analytical products built upon them.
