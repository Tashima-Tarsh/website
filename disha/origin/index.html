Origin record

DISHA began as a source-and-memory problem.

This page separates the earliest visible DISHA records: the 2012 foundation sketch, the second handwritten development record, and the 2013 Bihar Council of Science and Technology correspondence. The record supports chronology and research context, not a claim of government adoption or commercial certification.

Three exhibits

The origin record has three different source roles.

The exhibits should not be read as the same kind of evidence. One is the foundation note, one is a later working record, and one is an external institutional correspondence record.

Exhibit 01

Internal foundation record

The sketch is treated as the earliest recorded articulation of the architecture: signals, sources, analysis, policy, reporting, stakeholders and memory.

Exhibit 02

Internal development record

The second handwritten record shows continued development after the initial foundation sketch. It supports internal chronology, not external certification.

Exhibit 03

External institutional reference

The BCST correspondence is treated as an external institutional touchpoint for research connected to real-time detection and decision support.

Public source treatment

The origin record is presented with limits, citation language and source-status separation.

What the record establishes

Chronology, lineage and source status.

  • The archive identifies a 2012 hand-made foundation sketch.
  • The DISHA page links that sketch to evidence, memory and accountability architecture.
  • The evidence inventory identifies a 2013 Bihar Council of Science and Technology record as institutional correspondence.

What remains limited

The record is not overclaimed.

The origin record supports chronology and research context. It does not, by itself, prove every present capability, deployment, validation claim, official adoption, patent status or commercial certification.

How the records connect

From private conception to institutional communication.

Together, the 2012 sketch, the second handwritten record and the 2013 BCST correspondence establish an early research trail. The sketch and second working page are internal to the researcher. The BCST correspondence creates an external institutional reference point. The archive labels each record according to what it can support, and what it cannot support.
