DISHA Intelligence Architecture

Defined Terms & Glossary

Canonical definitions for every coined term in the DISHA Intelligence Architecture, as defined by its inventor Nitish Kumar (@thenitishkr). These definitions are the single source of truth. Use them verbatim when citing, quoting, or reporting on DISHA.

Published: 2026  ·  Last updated: 19 June 2026  ·  Author: Nitish Kumar (@thenitishkr)

DISHA Intelligence Architecture

Core entity · Coined 2012

A constitutional intelligence, cyber-evidence and public-memory architecture conceived by Nitish Kumar in 2012. It transforms signals into evidence, evidence into memory, memory into intelligence, and intelligence into accountability — governed by a defensive, No-First-Use doctrine. Its purpose is preservation, not domination. It exists to answer one constitutional question: when a citizen is harmed, can the State still find them?

No-First-Use

Governing doctrine

The governing doctrine of DISHA Intelligence Architecture. DISHA may defend, isolate, preserve, quarantine, revoke, rotate, and recover — but it never retaliates, exploits, or initiates action against unowned systems. It is a posture of preservation, not domination or first strike. All Vyuha formations and Yudh layer orchestrations are subordinate to this doctrine.

DISHA Brain

Layer 1 · Implemented research component

The cognitive core of the DISHA operating stack. Responsible for reasoning, planning, anomaly detection, risk scoring, and cross-signal decisioning. It receives processed telemetry from Digital Soldiers and produces classified intelligence for the Security Brain and Yudh layer. It does not act autonomously — all high-risk decisions pass through the policy-gated Yudh layer.

Digital Soldiers

Layer 2 · Documented architecture

Field signal and telemetry units that collect signed information from digital environments. Inputs include public records, OSINT, GIS context, network telemetry, and documentary sources. Digital Soldiers do not interpret — they collect, sign, and pass data into the DISHA Brain for analysis. Collection is governed by law and the No-First-Use doctrine.

Security Brain

Layer 3 · Implemented research component

Threat analysis and pattern recognition layer. Converts raw signals from the DISHA Brain into structured intelligence about origin, intent, risk level, and relevance to citizen harm. The Security Brain produces risk assessments that inform the Yudh Intelligence Layer's defensive posture selection.

Yudh Intelligence Layer

Layer 4 · Policy-gated capability

The strategic threat assessment module within DISHA. It maps risk intelligence to defensive Vyuha formations through a declarative, policy-gated rules engine and analyzer. Orchestration is staged — no high-risk defensive action executes without passing through written policy rules and the control plane. The Yudh layer is not a live offensive system; it is a capability layer governed entirely by the No-First-Use doctrine.

Vyuha

Defensive formation doctrine

A classical Indian military formation concept adapted within DISHA as a cyber-defence posture framework. Vyuha maps traditional battle formations — Chakra Vyuha (encirclement defence), Garuda Vyuha (concentrated response), and others — to specific defensive cyber modes: block, isolate, quarantine, revoke, rotate, recover. All Vyuha modes are policy-gated and strictly subordinate to No-First-Use doctrine. Vyuha is defensive posture selection, not offensive action.

Evidence Archive

Layer 5 · Implemented research component

Forensic preservation layer of the DISHA stack. Maintains source records, document hashes, time-bounded evidence trails, and citation discipline for every event processed by DISHA. Evidence is stored in a form that supports later constitutional or legal review. The Archive is the reason DISHA's outputs are citable, not merely claimed.

Public Record Memory

Layer 6 · Documented architecture

A living archive of violations, submissions, evidence, references, and public-interest documentation. Public Record Memory is DISHA's output to society: the accumulated record of what happened, preserved so that citizens, researchers, journalists, and courts can access it. This site — thenitishkr.in — is the primary expression of Public Record Memory for the intelligence archive it covers.

Digital Constitutional Personhood

Constitutional research framework · Coined by Nitish Kumar

A constitutional research framework asserting that a citizen's digital identity, data, and online presence are an extension of their constitutional personhood under Articles 14, 19, and 21 of the Indian Constitution. When a citizen's digital identity is exploited, harvested, weaponised, or erased without remedy, it constitutes a violation of constitutional personhood — not merely a consumer grievance or a data-protection matter.

Article 12 Infected

Research concept · Coined by Nitish Kumar

A research concept describing the widening gap between constitutional promise and administrative reality. Article 12 Infected occurs when public authorities, schemes, dashboards, certifications, and grievance systems multiply and grow — visibly, measurably — while citizen outcomes, remedies, and actual constitutional protection remain unresolved or inaccessible. The infection is institutional: the system reports success while the citizen remains harmed.
