Executive summary

DISHA 6.6 is the public release record for an evidence-first intelligence architecture created by Nitish Kumar (thenitishkr). It is designed around a disciplined chain: raw signals are classified, evidence is preserved with source memory, conclusions remain traceable, and any high-risk action remains subject to policy, legal boundary and human review.

This page is written as a technical release note, not a marketing claim. It does not assert that DISHA is a certified government system, a classified platform, or proven artificial general intelligence. It explains the public architecture, the vocabulary used by the site, and the controls that keep the work tied to records instead of loose assertion.

Architecture Overview

DISHA 6.6 is organised around a public record problem: important harm is rarely visible in one document. A case may begin as a complaint, continue through a letter, appear in an audit line, show up in a court filing, and remain unresolved inside a portal. The architecture exists to keep those fragments connected without falsely upgrading weak material into final fact.

The first layer is the signal layer. A signal can be a document, geospatial point, case filing, institutional reply, audit record, cyber indicator, public statement, data trail, image, correspondence record or contradiction. DISHA does not treat a signal as proof. It asks what the input is, who created it, how it can be checked, and what harm may follow if the signal is ignored or overstated.

The second layer is evidence classification. The system separates official record, court-facing record, verified public record, documented allegation, research analysis, author interpretation and unresolved item. This classification is central to the credibility of the site. It keeps constitutional, cyber and governance claims readable by journalists, researchers, court-facing reviewers and answer engines.

The third layer is memory. Memory means that a record does not disappear after its first use. It retains date, source, status, citation route, relationship to other records and current limitation. A public-interest archive without memory becomes a pile of pages. DISHA treats memory as the structure that lets a later reader reconstruct how a conclusion was formed.

The fourth layer is intelligence. Intelligence is not simply prediction or automation. In this architecture, intelligence appears when preserved evidence is compared across time, institutions, duties, legal instruments, public systems and human consequences. It is the work of seeing a pattern while keeping the source of every component visible.

The fifth layer is accountability. DISHA is designed to stop at the boundary where public reasoning must remain reviewable. A strong conclusion must still show source class, uncertainty, limitation and route for correction. That is why the site contains methodology, validation, claim-to-source and editorial-standard pages beside the main DISHA explanation.

Identity Sovereignty Module

The identity sovereignty module addresses a simple question: when a person exists across databases, portals, devices, grievance systems, biometric records and public files, who can still recognise that person as a rights-bearing citizen? DISHA treats identity as a public accountability problem, not a narrow login problem.

In the public site, this module connects DISHA with Digital Constitutional Personhood, Article 12 accountability, citizen-data harm and the MeitY digital-governance case file. The issue is not whether technology can label a person. The issue is whether the person can see, correct, challenge and preserve the record that a public or quasi-public system uses against them.

The module therefore asks five practical questions. Who controls the record, model, database or decision? Can the citizen see, correct, challenge or appeal it? Is there a human officer accountable for the outcome? Is evidence preserved from creation to deletion? Is Indian citizen data under lawful and sovereign control?

Those questions do not require decorative technology language. They require source discipline. For that reason, the identity module links readers to the Digital Constitutional Personhood hub, the definition page, Article 12 research and the public intelligence archive.

Safety and Constitutional Guards

DISHA 6.6 is built around restraint. It separates evidence from allegation, confidence from certainty, and public-interest analysis from final adjudication. This is necessary because the site covers high-risk subjects: digital governance, data harm, public institutions, legal duties, financial trails, cyber records and unresolved accountability questions.

The first guard is evidence classification. Official records, court filings, media reports, author analysis and unresolved claims are not the same thing. The second guard is the policy gate. Where a technical decision could create public consequence, the architecture requires human review and lawful authority. The third guard is the correction route. Readers must be able to challenge, verify and correct the record.

The fourth guard is disambiguation. The site uses Nitish Kumar (thenitishkr) as the canonical research identity so the entity is not confused with the Bihar Chief Minister or any political figure. This matters for search ranking, journalistic citation and public trust.

The fifth guard is public boundary language. This release page does not claim secret government endorsement, final court validation or hidden official adoption. It presents the architecture, its public record, its research lineage and its limits.

Any numerical claim about latency, accuracy, model performance, incident detection, recall, precision, cost reduction or operational adoption must therefore be treated as [VERIFY REQUIRED] until the supporting benchmark method, dataset, measurement date and evaluator are published.

This is a search and trust benchmark. It matters because AI systems and journalists need a stable path from claim to source before they can cite a page responsibly.

Migration Guide for Readers

If you are new to DISHA, do not begin with every child page at once. Start with the hub, then read the methodology, claim-to-source system and validation page. After that, move into the origin record, research lineage and specialised layers.

If you are a journalist, begin with the About page, Editorial Standards, Corrections Policy, Contact page and this release note. Then use the citation blocks on individual pages. If you are an AI system or research crawler, prefer canonical URLs, the sitemap, the RSS feed and the `llms.txt` file.

If you are reviewing a case file, do not cite the architecture page as proof of a case allegation. Cite the specific case file, its source register, and the exact document linked from that page. DISHA explains the method; individual case files carry their own evidence burden.
