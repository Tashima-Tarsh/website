Finding 01 / Section 1

Ultrasonic Audio Beacon Architecture (uXDT)

Ultrasonic audio beacons are inaudible high-frequency tones (18–22 kHz) embedded in TV advertisements, radio broadcasts, retail store speakers, website JavaScript, and in-app audio. Mobile phones can record up to 22 kHz using standard microphone hardware. Any app holding the RECORD_AUDIO permission can detect and decode these tones without the user's knowledge — a technique called Ultrasonic Cross-Device Tracking (uXDT).

SilverPush SDK Architecture — the dominant uXDT technology from 2014 to 2017 — sampled audio at 44,100 Hz, applied Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) analysis on 4,096-sample blocks, matched patterns at 18–20 kHz, and transmitted the beacon ID plus Android device GAID/IMEI to SilverPush servers. The server correlated beacon events with desktop cookies to determine cross-device identity without any account login.

Finding 02 / Section 2

AI Vision / Computer Vision Harvesting Architecture

The CAMERA permission grants access to the device's live video feed. While legitimately used in QR scanners and video call apps, it is also exploited to run continuous computer vision analysis in the background, harvesting biometric data without user awareness. Face++ SDK (by Megvii, Beijing) processes 106 facial landmarks per frame. At 5 fps — a low-power background monitoring rate — this produces 31,800 facial data points per minute.

Biometric data is permanent: Unlike passwords, you cannot change your face. Once facial geometry has been extracted and stored as a mathematical measurement set, it can be used to identify you forever — even if the original app is deleted. Facial geometry hashes are highly resistant to disguise and are increasingly used for cross-platform identity correlation without consent.

Finding 03 / Section 3

AdTech Real-Time Bidding (RTB) — Data Pipeline Architecture

When you open an app or visit a website, an auction for your personal data takes place in under 100 milliseconds. The ICCL (Irish Council for Civil Liberties) calls it "the biggest data breach ever recorded." RTB broadcasts personal data 294 billion times per day in the USA and 197 billion times per day in Europe (ICCL, 2022). Google's RTB system operates on 33.7 million websites, 92% of Android apps, and 77% of iOS apps.

Why RTB is structurally impossible to regulate: The IAB's own CEO acknowledged in a 2017 email (obtained via FOIA): "As it is technically impossible for the user to have prior information about every data controller involved in a real-time bidding scenario, [RTB] would seem, at least prima facie, to be incompatible with consent under GDPR." An IAB Tech Lab document explicitly states there are "no technical measures" to control what companies do with data once broadcast in a bid request. Belgium's DPA ruled in February 2022 that the IAB Europe's TCF consent framework itself breaches GDPR — the system continues operating. A 2023 Enforce report found RTB data from active US military personnel, national security leaders, and judges was available for commercial purchase. Companies with "Beijing" in their title appear on Google's certified RTB partner list.

Finding 04 / Section 4

AI Model Training on Your Data — The Subscription Loop

The most sophisticated exploitation of user data follows a five-stage cycle: offer a free service that collects data → use that data to train AI models → productise the AI model → sell access as a subscription → use subscription revenue to collect more data. The user is simultaneously the raw material and the eventual paying customer.

Regulatory gap (2026): No comprehensive global framework governs how AI models trained on user data must disclose training sources; whether users have rights to deletion from AI model weights (GDPR's right to erasure is practically impossible to implement in trained neural networks); whether AI-inferred attributes — health status, sexuality, political views — in adtech are regulated differently from explicitly collected attributes; or what constitutes adequate consent for biometric AI training. The EU AI Act classifies some uses as "high risk" but creates exemptions for advertising and commercial profiling.

Chinese law compels all Chinese companies and citizens to cooperate with state intelligence demands, regardless of where the company is registered or where data is stored.

Singapore Shell Architecture: Singapore became the preferred routing point for Chinese tech companies' international data operations because: it is not subject to Chinese data localisation laws on its face; it carries a trusted international reputation; its PDPA is less restrictive than GDPR; physical proximity to Chinese data centres enables low-latency mirroring; and Singapore-registered companies can open US App Store and Google Play developer accounts without triggering national security reviews. Despite the legal separation, China's National Intelligence Law extends to any Chinese citizen or organisation anywhere in the world — a Singapore-registered subsidiary of a Chinese parent is still subject to Chinese intelligence demands through the parent company relationship.

Finding 09 / Section 9

Regulatory Failures and What Actually Works

What has failed: GDPR (2018) — RTB still operates at 294 billion daily broadcasts; IAB TCF ruled GDPR-violating in 2022 yet system still runs; no major adtech company shut down for GDPR violations. CCPA (California, 2020) — data brokers still operate; opt-out mechanisms impractical at scale. FTC enforcement — InMobi fined USD 950K (trivial relative to revenues); TikTok/ByteDance lawsuit filed 2024, outcome pending. Google Play self-policing — reactive, not preventive; versioning attacks bypass initial review consistently.

What has actually worked:

  • Government bans with enforcement: India's ban of 400+ Chinese apps effectively removed them from Indian devices. US PAFACA enforcement removed TikTok from US app stores. More decisive than any regulatory fine.
  • Platform-level technical controls: Android 9 background camera/mic restriction genuinely reduced background surveillance. Android 10 background location separation was effective. Android 13 granular media permissions meaningfully reduced photo access.
  • Security research pressure: Citizen Lab, Braunschweig researchers, and Lookout publications forced Google to remove specific apps and SDKs faster than any regulatory action.
  • Google Play Protect: Real-time scanning of 200 billion apps daily provides a genuine technical barrier. Play Protect identified 13 million new malware threats outside Play Store in 2024.
  • Private Space (Android 15): Technically effective isolation of sensitive apps from app surveillance.
User-level protections that work: (1) Deny RECORD_AUDIO to any app that does not need it for a clear stated purpose — this is the gate for all audio beacon harvesting. (2) Deny CAMERA to shopping, finance, and news apps. (3) Never grant BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE to any app that is not a legitimate accessibility tool — this is the master key for near-total device control. (4) Check Android 12+ Privacy Dashboard (Settings → Privacy → Privacy Dashboard) to see which apps accessed mic, camera, and location in the past 24 hours. (5) Use one-time permissions whenever available. (6) Install SoniControl (open source) to detect and block ultrasonic beacon frequencies. (7) Treat APKPure as unsafe — the platform itself was compromised by Trojan malware in April 2021.

Sources: Citizen Lab, Braunschweig TU, ICCL, Brave, ICO, FTC, Kaspersky, Zimperium, Lookout, BuzzFeed News, EFF. Report date: April 2026. All statements are based on the source document; verify against primary agency records before citing in legal proceedings.

Finding 10 / Aadhaar Breach

Aadhaar Ecosystem Breach 2018–2026 — 1 Billion Records, 210 Portals, Zero Citizen Notification

On January 3, 2018, The Tribune newspaper published an investigation confirming that access to the details of any of the 1 billion+ Aadhaar holders could be purchased for Rs 500 via a WhatsApp contact. Within 10 minutes, the purchaser received access to a portal allowing searches by name, address, postal code, photo, date of birth, email, and phone number for any searched Aadhaar number. CERT-In investigation confirmed at least 210 government department and state government websites had been exposing Aadhaar data through misconfigured APIs and public-facing databases. UIDAI's response was to file an FIR against The Tribune reporter — widely condemned as criminalising responsible disclosure.

Critical action — lock your Aadhaar biometrics: As of 2024, fewer than 5% of Aadhaar holders have enabled biometric lock. UIDAI does not proactively notify citizens when their Aadhaar is used for authentication. Any Indian with Aadhaar-linked services remains vulnerable if their demographic data is in any of the documented breach datasets. Action: Immediately lock Aadhaar biometrics at uidai.gov.in or mAadhaar app. Security measures introduced post-2018: Virtual ID (16-digit temporary number); Aadhaar Biometric Lock (citizen-initiated); Masked Aadhaar (last 4 digits only — officially valid for most private uses); OTP-based authentication limit (10 attempts per day per Aadhaar). None of these are enabled by default.

Finding 11 / CoWIN Breach

CoWIN Health Data Compromise — 150 Million Vaccination Records on Telegram, June 2023

In June 2023, India's CoWIN portal — the government's COVID-19 vaccine management platform — suffered a data exposure placing the vaccination records, Aadhaar numbers, and mobile numbers of over 150 million Indian citizens on Telegram as a searchable bot. This was not a minor leak: it was a bot that allowed anyone to query specific individuals' health records using their phone number or Aadhaar number. The Ministry of Health stated: 'The CoWIN portal is completely safe. No data breach has occurred.' CloudSEK researchers sampled 100 queried records and found 100% accuracy rate. No individual or organisation has been criminally charged for the CoWIN breach as of 2026.

Why this breach is more serious than disclosed: The government's position that 'no central breach occurred' is technically narrow but misleading. A government vaccination database is uniquely dangerous because: (1) it contains Aadhaar linkage for virtually every adult Indian; (2) the data was mandatory — citizens had no opt-out; (3) it includes children's data through family registration; (4) it was used to create one of the most comprehensive identity + health + location databases ever assembled in India. Data volume: researchers estimated the full dataset at 150–200 million unique records. As of Q1 2024, data is live on BreachForums and multiple dark web markets, being sold for $3,000–$8,000 per million records. Criminal exploitation potential includes: digital arrest profiling (vaccination centre location reveals physical whereabouts of high-value targets), health insurance fraud, medical record tampering, and identity verification for social engineering scams.

Pattern across all breaches: Government denial → third-party vector finding → no arrests → no citizen notification → data remains on dark web markets. The BSNL breach (278 GB of IMSI/SIM data) is particularly critical because it enables industrial-scale SIM cloning — making every SIM swap fraud exponentially easier. The aggregate effect is that every Indian who has registered a phone, received a vaccine, filed an income tax return, used a health service, ordered food, or booked a ticket has had their data breached at least once and likely multiple times.
