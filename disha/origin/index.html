Origin record

DISHA Origin Record

The DISHA origin record is presented through two distinct source points: a foundation sketch dated 25 April 2012 and a later 2013 Bihar Council of Science & Technology correspondence record. The page does not treat either record as a commercial certification.

What the record establishes

  • the archive identifies a 2012 hand-made foundation sketch;
  • the DISHA page links that sketch to the evidence, memory and accountability architecture;
  • the evidence inventory lists a 2013 Bihar Council of Science & Technology record as institutional correspondence.

What remains limited

The origin record supports chronology and research context. It does not, by itself, prove every present capability, deployment or validation claim.

The 2012 foundation sketch

The foundation sketch dated 25 April 2012 represents the earliest recorded articulation of the intelligence architecture that would later be named DISHA. At that stage, the core concept was a system to transform raw signals — observations, records, communications intercepts and open-source data — into structured, accountable intelligence memory. The sketch was hand-drawn and hand-dated, consistent with early-stage conceptual work by an individual researcher operating outside institutional infrastructure. The archive treats the sketch as a chronological anchor, not as a patent filing or certified invention record.

The significance of the 2012 date is that it predates the institutionalisation of many of the governance, cyber-security and constitutional accountability debates that DISHA would later address directly. When Nitish Kumar developed the first sketch, India's conversation about digital governance, biometric identity systems and data sovereignty was far less advanced than it is today. The origin record therefore demonstrates that the DISHA framework was conceived as a constitutional-intelligence response to emerging challenges, not as a retrospective commentary on problems already resolved.

The 2013 Bihar Council of Science and Technology record

The Bihar Council of Science and Technology (BCST) correspondence in 2013 marks the first institutional touchpoint for the research. The submission concerned real-time explosive detection, an area in which the DISHA architecture's core method — signals to evidence to memory to actionable intelligence — had direct application. The council record demonstrates that the research was communicated to a public scientific body at a defined date, establishing a documented institutional trail independent of any commercial or proprietary filing.

The archive does not assert that the BCST submission resulted in a formal programme adoption or government deployment. The record is treated as institutional correspondence that adds to the chronology of the DISHA research lineage. For public-interest research purposes, the existence of the submission is itself significant: it shows that the framework was being presented to institutions within twelve months of the foundation sketch, before the architecture had been named DISHA and before the broader constitutional-accountability dimensions of the research had been fully elaborated.

How the two records connect

Together, the 2012 sketch and the 2013 BCST record establish a two-year early history for the DISHA project. The sketch is internal to the researcher; the BCST submission is external, producing an institutional reference point. Both are available in the evidence inventory. The archive methodology requires that these records be labelled with their correct source status: the sketch is a verified internal record and the BCST correspondence is verified institutional correspondence. Neither record constitutes government endorsement, commercial licensing or validated deployment confirmation.

Subsequent development of the DISHA architecture — including the definition of the six layers (L1 DISHA Brain through L6 Public Record Memory), the No-First-Use doctrine, and the constitutional-intelligence framing that connects the research to Articles 12, 14, 19 and 21 of the Indian Constitution — built on this foundation across more than a decade of additional work, culminating in the Supreme Court petitions filed in 2025 and 2026.
